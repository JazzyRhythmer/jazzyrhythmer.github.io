    A DIRECT WAY INTO THE SOUND

    Play first.
    Go deeper when you need to.

    PLAY

    Eight main controls and an engine-specific animation that responds as you play. Choose a preset and make it your own.

    EDIT

    Sound, modulation and effects controls, grouped by purpose. Up to sixteen controls share each compact EDIT page. Related groups stay together, with the current page always visible.

    RHYTHMER KIT

    A kit to start.
    A sound to change.

    Play all sixteen pads. Choose a complete kit, then replace a pad’s engine or sound. Shape the selected pad and use the kit filter to bring the whole drum mix together.

    The starting kit is K-WASUB / 909. Kit levels are balanced around that reference, with a peak guard for the combined output.

    GEN / MIDI PATTERNS

    Find a pattern.
    Keep it moving.

    Generate a melody, a chord progression or a drum pattern. Choose a genre, key, scale and length. Explore Block, Arp and Strum chord styles, then try a variation.

    Drag MIDI into your DAW or export a MIDI file. Edit and play the clip there, at your project’s tempo. GEN creates the notes; your DAW handles playback.

    YOUR FIRST SESSION

    From a sound
    to a part.

    LOAD

    Add the instrument.

    Install the AU, VST2 or VST3 bundle, then load Rhythmer Engines on a MIDI instrument track. Open its custom editor. Rhythmer Kit / K-WASUB / 909 is the starting sound.

    PLAY

    Find your sound.

    Play the kit’s pads from notes 36–51 (C1–D♯2 when C3 is note 60), or choose a synth with ENGINE. Use PRESET to browse that engine’s sounds.

    SHAPE

    Make it yours.

    Use the eight PLAY controls for the main sound. Open EDIT for the full controls. In a kit, select a pad before changing its sound or parameters.

    GENERATE

    Take a pattern back.

    Open GEN, choose Melody or Chords for a synth, or Drums for a kit. Generate, inspect the notes, then drag MIDI into a DAW clip lane. Use Export MIDI if your host does not accept a file drag.

    More about playback, saving and MIDI

    Incoming MIDI velocity reaches the instrument. MIDI GEN keeps note velocities and lengths in the exported file. Save your DAW project to retain the engine, sound edits, kit routing and GEN pattern. STOP SOUND silences held sound; DAW transport stop also clears held notes and sustain.

    GEN uses 4/4 and writes no tempo change into its MIDI files. It does not run a second sequencer or provide a real-time MIDI-output port. For Block chords, choose a polyphonic sound; Arp also works with monophonic sounds.

    DAWs use different octave names. MIDI note numbers are the reliable reference for the kit: 36–51.

    INSTALLATION / COPY THE COMPLETE BUNDLES

    Copy. Rescan. Play.

    Extract the downloaded ZIP and quit your DAW. Use Finder’s Go → Go to Folder (Shift–Command–G) to open the destination below.

    Audio Unit / AU

    Copy this complete bundle:

    AU/Rhythmer Engines.component

    Into this folder:

    ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/Components/

    VST2

    Copy this complete bundle:

    VST2/Rhythmer Engines.vst

    Into this folder:

    ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/VST/

    VST3

    Copy this complete bundle:

    VST3/Rhythmer Engines.vst3

    Into this folder:

    ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/VST3/
    1. Create the destination folders if needed. Install any format supported by your DAW.
    2. Copy each whole .component, .vst or .vst3 bundle. Keep its contents together; do not copy individual files from inside it.
    3. Reopen your DAW and rescan plug-ins. Enable AU, VST2 or VST3 in the host’s plug-in settings if needed.
    4. Load Rhythmer Engines on a MIDI instrument track. Rhythmer Kit / K-WASUB / 909 is the starting sound. Play MIDI notes 36–51 for its pads, or choose an individual synth with ENGINE.

    Keep one installation location for each format. If you have an older test copy in /Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/, back it up outside the plug-in folders before installing this user-folder copy.

    If the plug-in does not appear or load

    If a folder is missing, open ~/Library/ in Finder and create Audio, then Plug-Ins, then Components, VST or VST3 inside it.

    • Check the folder and format: the AU bundle belongs in Components; the VST2 bundle belongs in VST; the VST3 bundle belongs in VST3.
    • Use the native Apple Silicon version of your DAW and rescan. This download contains arm64 builds.
    • Check for an older duplicate under /Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/ as well as your user Library.
    • This beta uses ad-hoc signing, without Developer ID or Apple notarization. Some systems or hosts may block it. For a macOS security message, review Apple’s Privacy & Security guidance. If no loading option is available, contact me with your macOS and DAW version and the exact message.
